Thermal Control of CubeSats using Micro-structured Films

a passive film, modeled on the Saharan silver ant, to keep CubeSat electronics cool

project
02 of 05
event
NASA SEES
credit
Research conducted through the NASA STEM Enhancement in Earth Science internship, mentored by Bridget Kimsey.
links
Read the paper →

01the problem

  • CubeSats are shoebox sized with almost no mass budget for cooling hardware
  • AI chips on board run hot and absorbed sunlight adds to the load
  • radiation flips bits in memory and corrupts what the chip computes
  • radiation hardened parts exist but lag commercial chips badly on performance

02the mechanism

  • the Saharan silver ant survives midday desert heat with no active cooling
  • its hairs have a triangular cross section that drives total internal reflection
  • the same geometry raises emission in the mid infrared
  • reflecting and radiating at once is exactly what a satellite face needs

03the proposed film

  • replicate that triangular microstructure in a thin silicone film
  • mount on the minus Z face and couple it to the internal electronics
  • passive throughout, no power draw and almost no added mass

04results

  • research conducted through the NASA STEM Enhancement in Earth Science Internship
  • paper published on SSRN and available to read
  • literature supports solar reflectance above 95 percent for this structure class
  • passive throughout, so it draws no power and adds almost no mass to the satellite
