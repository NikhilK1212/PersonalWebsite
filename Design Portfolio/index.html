01. A Comprehensive Machine Learning Paradigm for Space Debris Surveillance

Research project with one partner, Nived Nandakumar. Presented at International Science & Engineering Fair 2025, awarded 4th place in Physics and Astronomy category. Published on SSRN. Read the paper

The problem

  • Millions of fragments from old rockets and dead satellites now orbit Earth
  • Kessler syndrome is the runaway case, where each collision has a cascading effect and creates more debris
  • Ground telescopes can only track the larger pieces due to atmosphere distortion
  • No system has actually removed debris, only proposals and planned missions

Model one, detection

  • Convolutional neural network trained on over 5,000 labelled orbital images
  • Multi task loss combining classification, localization and confidence
  • Anchor boxes refined against ground truth by minimizing intersection over union
  • Random scaling, rotation and flipping to generalize

Model two, trajectory

  • Physics-informed neural network with an Long Short Term Memory layer reading trajectory history
  • Kepler's third law, eccentricity limits, drag, and gravity all enforced as loss terms
  • A non physics-informed neural network wouldn't be able to constrain the orbits to something that is only physically possible
  • Time derivatives modelled with gradient tape so predictions stay smooth

Model three, collision risk

  • Random forest regressor, 100 decision trees on bootstrapped samples
  • Works from relative speed, distance, and approach angle
  • Risk predicted on a log base 2 scale for resolution at low probabilities
  • Averaging decorrelated trees cuts variance, which the formula makes explicit

The laser application

  • The three models feed a proposed satellite carrying a pulsed laser
  • Focused heat vaporizes a thin surface layer, which is ablation
  • The plasma plume pushes the fragment into a lower orbit until it burns up
  • More precise than any ground-based attempt because the laser system is directly integrated with the machine learning famework

Results

  • Detection reached 95.4% mean average precision and 98% recall confidence
  • Trajectory model reached R-squared 0.979 with mean squared error 0.015
  • Collision risk model reached R-squared 0.981 with mean squared error 0.014
  • All three run behind one interface: upload an image and get object, orbit, and risk together

02. Thermal Control of CubeSats using Micro-structured Films

Research conducted through the NASA STEM Enhancement in Earth Science Internship, mentored by Bridget Kimsey. Paper published on SSRN. Read the paper

The problem

  • CubeSats are shoebox-sized with almost no mass budget for cooling hardware
  • AI chips on board run hot and absorbed sunlight adds to the load
  • Radiation flips bits in memory and corrupts what the chip computes
  • Radiation-hardened chips exist but lag commercial chips badly on performance

The mechanism

  • The Saharan silver ant survives midday desert heat with no active cooling
  • Its hairs have a triangular cross section that drives total internal reflection
  • The same geometry raises emission in the mid-infrared
  • Reflecting and radiating out simultaneously is what a satellite face needs to regulate temperature

The proposed film

  • Replicate that triangular microstructure in a thin silicone film
  • Mount on the minus Z face and couple it to the internal electronics
  • Passive throughout, no power draw and almost no added mass

Results

  • Paper published on SSRN. Read the paper

  • Literature supports solar reflectance above 95% for this structure class
  • Passive throughout, so it draws no power and adds almost no mass to the satellite

03. Penn Electric Racing Mechanical Challenge: Brake Design

Solo design project, completed in one week as the mechanical application challenge for Penn Electric Racing.

The problem

  • Formula SAE is a student contest to design and build a race car
  • This was the Penn mechanical application challenge, which I did alone
  • The rules demand two independent hydraulic circuits and no brake by wire
  • Target pressures of 1200 psi front and 400 psi rear had to be validated, not assumed

From deceleration to hydraulic pressure

  • Weight transfer under 1.5 g braking moves 740.5 N onto the front axle
  • Front axle load rises to 1964.8 N while the rear drops to 585.8 N
  • Tire friction sets the torque each axle must absorb, 748.6 and 223.2 N⋅m
  • Working back through the caliper gives 613 psi front and 183 psi rear required

Pedal and hydraulics

  • Moment balance about the pivot gives a pedal ratio of 6 to 1
  • A 1.0 inch pushrod arm therefore sets a 6.0 inch pedal arm, inside the envelope
  • 70 lbf of foot force becomes 420 lbf at the pushrod
  • One bore serves both circuits at 0.578 inch calculated, rounded to a 9/16 inch
  • Balance bar pivot at one quarter length splits that 420 lbf into 315 and 105 lbf

Structural sizing

  • Structural case uses the rulebook 1500 N foot load, giving 2023 lbf at the pushrod
  • Bending stress with a safety factor of 2.5 sizes the aluminium arm to 0.625 inch
  • Steel would need only 0.448 inch but weighs more for the stiffness gained, so aluminium wins
  • Euler buckling on the pushrod returns a safety factor of 48.2
  • Pin shear and bolt bearing return a safety factor of 3.24 and 54.1

Results

  • Full assembly specified with a bill of materials split into off the shelf and manufactured
  • Pedal sweeps 14.9 degrees over 1.6 inches of travel
  • Every structural member clears its safety factor with margin
  • Two independent circuits mean a failure on one axle still leaves braking on the other, creating redundancy

04. RIPPLE: AI-Powered Vertical Supply Chain Management for Medicine

Built over one weekend at DNHacks 2026 with four teammates. Showcase and source

The problem

  • Most medicines depend on ingredients from a handful of overseas factories
  • Six antibiotics that look diversified trace back to one shared precursor
  • If that factory stops, all six run short at once

The graph and the risk model

  • Public FDA registers pulled into one graph of 1,732 nodes and 3,425 edges
  • Failure spreads through the graph by breadth first search with a redundancy rollup
  • An L2 regularized logistic model scores which plant fails next, from nine leakage free features
  • Raw scores recalibrated so a displayed percentage matches observed frequency

Rerouting and the sensor

  • Switch off any factory and watch the disruption propagate
  • Replacement suppliers ranked on capability, geography and their own risk
  • Each checked against Trade Agreements Act and Defence Department restrictions
  • An ESP32 sensor streams cold chain telemetry and computes mean kinetic temperature

Results

  • Model reached 0.814 AUC against a 0.70 baseline, beating it on all three time cutoffs
  • Gradient boosting was tested and lost at 0.641, so the transparent model is also the accurate one
  • Supplier name matching scored 100% on a labelled benchmark
  • Covers 1,706 of 1,732 nodes, and the 26 it cannot resolve are left blank

05. G.E.A.R.: Go-Kart Efficiency through Aerodynamic Research

Senior research project with two classmates, presented at tjSTAR.

The problem

  • Open wheel karts churn the air around exposed tires creating drag instead and lose speed to it
  • The front panel is the first surface the airflow meets
  • We asked whether shaping it could cut drag without losing the downforce that creates grip

What governs the problem

  • Drag rises with the square of velocity, so losses grow fast at speed
  • Downforce follows the same square law, which is why the two trade against each other
  • Reynolds number sets how turbulent the flow around the kart becomes
  • Goal was a panel that performs inside a high Reynolds number regime

How we tested

  • Several panel shapes designed in CAD: curved, flat, and sloped
  • Cowl and cowl-less variants to isolate how much drag the driver adds
  • Each was 3D printed and run in a wind tunnel against the bare chassis
  • Drag and front and rear downforce measured across a swept speed range

Results

  • The open panel beat the covered panel at every wind speed tested
  • The driver cover added more drag than the downforce it recovered was worth
  • The gap widened with speed, matching the square law
  • Computational Fluid Dynamics agreed with the findings from wind tunnel testing
